Pinellas County 2026 General Election: Every Candidate, Judge & Ballot Question

This guide covers everything that can appear on a Pinellas County ballot on November 3, 2026: the U.S. Senate special election, Governor and the Florida Cabinet, Congress, the Legislature, County Commission, School Board, fire districts, municipal offices, five judicial merit-retention votes, three statewide constitutional amendments, and fifteen city and town questions. Each candidate profile covers polling, money and backers, ideological lane, LGBTQ+ record, platform, controversies and endorsements, with sources.

Not every contest appears on every ballot. Congressional, legislative, county-district, fire-district and municipal contests depend on where you live. Unopposed candidates are not printed on the ballot. Check your sample ballot at VotePinellas.gov. The primary-election edition of this guide is preserved at index_primary.html.

Framework & Classification

🏷️ How to Read This Guide

Unlike the August primary, the general election puts Republicans, Democrats, Libertarians, independents and write-ins on the same ballot. Each candidate is placed in one of five ideological lanes based on party, funding sources, endorsement networks and policy record, and given an LGBTQ+ stance rating based on votes, statements and endorsements. These are editorial judgments; the sources behind each profile are listed in the candidate's full profile.

Progressive

Left of the Democratic establishment. Labor, grassroots and progressive-PAC backing; civil rights, housing justice, Medicare for All, explicit LGBTQ+ advocacy.

Establishment

Mainstream Democratic and center-left institutional candidates. Party committees, trade groups, pragmatic governance, caucus loyalty.

Conservative Establishment

Republican and conservative candidates. State GOP machinery, Chamber and industry PACs, culture-war groups such as Moms for Liberty; parental rights, tax cuts, restrictions on LGBTQ+ curricula and care.

Independent / Third Party

Libertarian (LPF), No Party Affiliation (NPA) and write-in candidates. Usually self-funded, rarely polled, platforms range from libertarian to populist.

Nonpartisan / Moderate

Hyper-local civic figures in nonpartisan seats (fire districts, small-city councils) focused on services, stormwater and public safety, with no clear partisan lane.

LGBTQ+ stance key: 🏳️‍🌈 Vanguard: outspoken champion or openly LGBTQ+ ✅ Consistent Support: pro-equality record or endorsements ⚖️ Neutral / Standard: no public record found ⚠️ Opposed / Culture War: restrictive votes, pledges or backers

Merit Retention · Yes / No

⚖️ Judicial Merit Retention

Florida appellate judges do not run against opponents. Voters decide "Shall Justice/Judge X be retained in office?" A judge who falls below 50% "Yes" loses the seat and the Governor appoints a replacement. No Florida appellate judge has ever lost a retention vote. The Florida Bar discontinued its merit-retention poll of lawyers after 2022, so the most recent Bar poll results (2020) are shown for each judge.
